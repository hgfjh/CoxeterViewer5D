# Glossary

These definitions describe the objects shown by the current viewer. A term can
have broader uses elsewhere in the Coxeter-group literature.

## Visual Quick Reference

| Term          | What the viewer shows                                                                                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cayley graph  | Group elements joined by right multiplication by a generator.                                                 |
| Davis cell    | A cell associated to a spherical special subgroup; a finite pair with order `m` gives a `2m`-gon.             |
| `hat X`       | A finite cover of the standard Coxeter presentation 2-complex, reconstructed from a complete finite action.   |
| `bar X`       | The even-sided complex obtained by compressing generator bigons and duplicate relation-cell lifts in `hat X`. |
| Wall          | An equivalence class of `bar X` edges generated by being opposite in a relation polygon.                      |
| Coorientation | A consistent choice of transverse direction on every edge dual to one two-sided wall.                         |
| Lawful cell   | A relation polygon whose directed boundary is the union of two positive paths from one source to one sink.    |
| Gamma         | The defining Coxeter graph: one vertex per generator and one edge per finite rank-two relation.               |
| Projection    | Chamber barycenters drawn in three dimensions from supplied reflection data.                                  |

## Standard Presentation Complex `X`

The one-vertex presentation 2-complex for the Coxeter presentation. It has one
oriented loop for each generator, a bigon for each relation `s_i^2`, and a
`2m_ij`-gon for each finite relation `(s_i s_j)^m_ij`. This is the base of the
finite cover used in the wall construction; it is not a separate model in the
current switch.

## Finite Cover `hat X`

Given a complete finite permutation action of the Coxeter group, the viewer
reconstructs the corresponding lift of the standard presentation complex.
There is one lifted vertex for each action point, one directed generator lift
and one lifted generator bigon from every vertex, and one lifted relation cell
per finite pair from every vertex. The two bigons based at the endpoints of a
generator orbit share their two boundary edges but remain distinct cells. A
finite action creates a cellular cover, but it does not by itself certify that
the corresponding subgroup is torsion-free.

## Compression `bar X`

The complex obtained from `hat X` by collapsing each pair of generator bigons
over a two-point generator orbit to one geometric edge and identifying the
`2m_ij` relation-cell lifts in each finite-dihedral orbit. The compression map
stores every cell fiber; it is not inferred from the 3D drawing.

## Wall

In an even-sided polygon, two boundary occurrences are opposite when their
indices differ by half the boundary length. The transitive closure of this
relation on the edges of `bar X` is an abstract wall. The drawn line through a
cell merely joins the relevant edge midpoints; the exact wall is the incidence
record.

## Embedded, Two-Sided, Self-Osculating

These are separate wall diagnostics. An embedded wall does not cross the same
cell in an inadmissible repeated way. A two-sided wall admits a globally
consistent transverse direction. A wall self-osculates when it is adjacent to
one vertex through more than one feature of that vertex link. Failed checks
retain witnesses for inspection.

## Wall Coorientation

A choice of sign for each two-sided wall. Parity constraints propagate that
choice to directions on all dual edges. Reversing one wall reverses every edge
direction belonging to that wall; a wall need not consist of edges carrying
one generator label.

## Lawful Cell And Lawful Subcomplex

Read the cooriented edge directions around a relation polygon. The cell is
lawful exactly when the cyclic sign word has two transitions, yielding one
source, one sink, and two positive boundary paths. For a fixed coorientation,
the lawful subcomplex contains the full 1-skeleton and every lawful 2-cell; it
is therefore canonical and maximal for that coorientation. Optimizing over all
coorientations is a different finite search problem.

## Coface-Closed Removal / Downward-Closed Retained Complex

In the generalized lawful construction, every unlawful 2-cell and all of its
higher cofaces form an upward-closed discarded set. The cells that remain are
therefore downward closed, which is the actual subcomplex condition. The short
phrase “coface-closed lawful construction” refers to closing the removal under
cofaces; it does not mean that the retained cells are upward closed.

## Ascending And Descending Links

At a vertex of the lawful subcomplex, an incident edge contributes to the
ascending link when it points away from the vertex and to the descending link
when it points toward the vertex. Corners of retained lawful cells give link
edges. Nonempty and connected links are exact finite diagnostics for the
chosen data, not by themselves a Morse or incoherence theorem.

## Cayley Graph And Davis Complex

The Cayley graph has vertices `w` and generator-labeled edges
`w --s_i--> w s_i`. The Davis complex attaches cells for spherical special
subgroups. A finite-radius Davis view can clip cells at its boundary.

## Defining Graph `Gamma`

The graph with one vertex for each Coxeter generator. The viewer draws every
finite off-diagonal entry, including `m = 2`, and labels the edge by `m`.
Infinite entries are omitted because they record no finite rank-two relation.

## Geometric Projection

A 3D drawing obtained from hyperbolic reflection data. Klein and Poincare maps
produce full-dimensional model coordinates; selecting three axes or applying
PCA can then lose metric and incidence visibility. Projection status must not
be read as an exact embedded 3D complex.

## Certified Data, Exact Incidence, Browser Diagnostic, Drawing

- **Certified data** has an attached artifact, hashes, backend, and named
  certificate scope.
- **Exact incidence** is finite combinatorial data accepted by the in-repo
  validators, such as signed boundaries and compression fibers.
- **Browser diagnostic** is a deterministic finite calculation without an
  external theorem certificate, such as a wall pathology check.
- **Drawing** covers coordinates, offsets, opacity, clipping, and camera state.

## Maximum And Best Found

**Maximum proven** means an exhaustive or branch-and-bound search completed and
its lower and upper bounds agree. **Best found** reports a feasible
coorientation and a lower bound after a wall, node, or time limit; it is not a
claim of optimality.

## Torsion-Free Evidence

Evidence that the subgroup defining a finite cover has no torsion in the
relevant scope. A valid permutation action and a passed compression certificate
do not imply torsion-freeness. The UI reports these statuses separately.

For an automatically discovered Coxeter-group cover, the preferred evidence is
a complete list of prime-order torsion representatives from spherical special
subgroups together with a proof that none fixes a point in the coset action.

## Tits Torsion Criterion

Every finite-order element of a Coxeter group is conjugate into a finite
spherical special subgroup. Combined with the prime-order fixed-point test,
this turns torsion-freeness of a finite transitive action into a finite
calculation, provided the representative list is complete.

## Bounded Cover Search

A low-index subgroup search performed only through a stated index and resource
budget. **Exhausted through index N** rules out an eligible subgroup only in
that range. **Inconclusive** means the computation stopped early. Neither means
that no finite-index torsion-free subgroup exists.

## Reidemeister--Schreier Presentation

A presentation of a subgroup `H <= G` obtained from a presentation of `G` and
coset representatives for `H`. The procedure constructs subgroup generators,
then rewrites every conjugate of every defining relator of `G` in those new
generators. For the exact convention and source references, see
[Certifying a virtual algebraic fibration](virtual-algebraic-fibering.md#1-present-the-subgroup-h).

## Schreier Generator

For a chosen coset representative `t_q` and original generator `s_i`, the
Schreier generator is

```text
x_(q,i) = t_q s_i t_(q.s_i)^(-1).
```

It is the based loop that goes from the root to `q` in the transversal tree,
crosses the `s_i` edge, and returns through the tree. Tree-edge loops are
trivial; retained non-tree loops generate the subgroup. These are not the
original Coxeter generators and need not be a minimal generating set.

## Primitive Homomorphism And Normalization

A homomorphism `phi: H -> Z` is primitive when its image is all of `Z`. If a
nonzero raw wall map `chi` has image `dZ`, divide all **group periods** by `d`
to obtain `phi = chi/d`. This leaves the kernel and up/down directions
unchanged. It does not divide the individual `+1` and `-1` wall arrows.

## Virtual Algebraic Fibration

For this project, an explicit finite-index subgroup `H` together with an
epimorphism `H -> Z` whose kernel is finitely generated. The wall
coorientation supplies a candidate cellular cocycle. Torsion-free cover
evidence, primitivity, relation sums, and the applicable Morse-link hypotheses
must be checked separately.
